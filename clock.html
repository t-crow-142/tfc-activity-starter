The ClockTower Summary The ClockTower is the University of Auckland's most iconic landmark and one of the city's best-known heritage buildings. Originally built as the Old Arts Building and officially opened in 1926, it was the University's first permanent purpose-built home. Designed by architect Roy Alstan Lippincott in a distinctive Gothic-inspired style, the 54-metre tower was influenced by Oxford University's Tom Tower and quickly became a symbol of higher education in Auckland. Over the decades, the building housed Arts, Law, Architecture, Music, the University's library, and student facilities. Following extensive restoration and strengthening in the 1980s, it became the administrative heart of the University and now houses the Vice-Chancellor's offices, the Great Hall, and Council Room. Its stone façade, decorative carvings of native flora and fauna, and prominent position overlooking Albert Park make it one of Auckland's most photographed buildings.
